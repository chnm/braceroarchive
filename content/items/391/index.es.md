        Biographical Synopsis of Interviewee:  Jesús Téllez Sánchez was born on September 20, 1934, in Villa López, Chihuahua, México; his father, Melesio Téllez Talamantes, worked in agriculture, and his mother, Luisa Sánchez, was a housewife; Jesús was the eighth born of his four sisters and five brothers; he was formally educated through the fourth grade; by the time he was fifteen years old, he was caring for animals and working the land; in 1953, he enlisted in the bracero program; as a bracero, he labored in the fields of Colorado, Nebraska, Texas, and Wyoming; he continued working with the program until 1958. <br/>
        <br/>
        Summary of Interview:  Mr. Tellez recalls his hometown, going to school, and working with his father; in 1953, he was able to get on the list of eligible workers in order to enlist in the bracero program; he went through the contracting center in Chihuahua, Chihuahua, México; from there he was transported by train to El Paso, Texas, where he was medically examined, which including being stripped and deloused; the men who fumigated the braceros, however, wore uniforms for protection as well as masks; while at the processing center in Rio Vista, he was fed eggs, beans, and tortillas; before leaving for his worksite, he was given a sack lunch consisting of two sandwiches and an orange; as a bracero, he labored in the fields of Colorado, Nebraska, Texas, and Wyoming, and he continued working with the program until 1958; he goes on to detail housing, living arrangements, amenities, provisions, daily routines, work duties, payments, remittances, and recreational activities; his longest contract was in Pecos, Texas, where he worked with roughly five or six other braceros; they made their own food, and each paid about $5.00 per week for their share of food; a few years after becoming a bracero he married a woman from his hometown; he briefly describes their relationship, including correspondence while he was in the United States, and their six children; overall, he has positive memories of the program; one of his daughters later helped him become a legal resident.<br/>

        <br/>
        JT:	¿Sí?<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Se ve muy saludable.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	No se ve…<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos a ver.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Sí, se mira fuerte todavía. ¿Cómo regresó cuando terminó su contrato en el [19]58? ¿Cómo regresó a México?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	¿Pa atrás?<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pues en camión.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿El patrón le pagó el viaje?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	No, nomás hasta a El Paso nos traen y luego de ahí pa allá todo lo paga uno.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿El patrón lo lleva hasta El Paso?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Lo llevó, perdón.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Hasta Churobisco, ¿cómo? Churobisco, ¿cómo se llamaba la contratación ahí?<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Río Grande.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Churobisco, aquí en El Paso.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Churubusco?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Churubisca, ¿qué? ¿Cómo?<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	O, ¿el Chamizal?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Por ahí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Y ya casi vamos a terminar, pero, ¿qué significa el término de bracero para usted?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos que había acabado el trabajo y le daban un papel. No encontraron una mica. Yo, a mí me dieron dos micas. Nomás que se me perdió una y la otra no supe ni qué fue.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Y, ¿usted qué siente que lo llamen bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Gusto, porque andaba uno trabajando.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Sus recuerdos como bracero, ¿fueron lo mayoría positivos, negativos?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Positivos.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Positivos. ¿El haber sido bracero cambió su vida de alguna manera? ¿Cómo?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos allá en donde vivía uno, pos no salía uno pa ninguna parte. Aquí de perdida conocía uno algo y agarraba más dinero.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Le llegó a mandar a su esposa dinero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	De, ¿de cuando andaba de bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Cuando era su novia?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	También. (risas)<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Alguna otra cosa que recuerde de bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pues no, nomás cuando llevábanos ropa nueva.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Pa México.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pa México.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿A quién le llevaba ropa? <br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos a la… Mandaba vestidos, este, pa hacer vestidos.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Tela?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Tela.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Para su mamá? <br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pa, pa ella y pa los hijos míos, pa su hija, taba chiquita.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Cuando era bracero, ¿ya tenía hijos?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Ya.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Ya.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos, no le digo que me casé como al año que entré para acá.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¡Ah! Entonces, ¿cuántos hijos tenía cuando era bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Tenía tres.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Tres. O sea, se casó en el [19]54.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Oh. Yo me ima… Yo pensé que en el [19]59.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	No.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Entonces, recién, recién que se casó…<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Me vine pa acá.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Aquí trabajaba.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Y, ¿iba a visitarlos seguido? <br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Sí? Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Claro que, pos, acababa el contrato.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Y regresaba.<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pa atrás.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¡Ah! Y, ¿qué le decía su esposa?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos que ya, ya había venido, que le daba gusto.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Fue difícil para ella cuando usted andaba de bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos en partes, en ratos.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Por qué?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Pos porque lo echaba menos. <br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	 (risas) Y, ¿usted a ella?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	También.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Sí. Entonces, ¿se comunicaban por carta siempre?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Y a ella era la que también, pues le mandaba dinero a ella y a sus papás. ¿Algún otro hermano suyo fue bracero?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Familiares?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Como dos o tres que vinieron.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Dos, dos o tres hermanos suyos. <br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	También vinieron.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Tíos?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	No.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	¿Primos?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	No.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	No. Que usted sepa, no. Muchos de sus amigos, ¿vinieron?<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Sí, conocidos.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Bueno, pues creo que ya…<br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	Ya es hora.<br/>
        <br/>
        VM:	Hemos terminado. <br/>
        <br/>
        JT:	¿Sí?<br/>

Biographical Synopsis of Interviewee:  Jesús Téllez Sánchez was born on September 20, 1934, in Villa López, Chihuahua, México; his father, Melesio Téllez Talamantes, worked in agriculture, and his mother, Luisa Sánchez, was a housewife; Jesús was the eighth born of his four sisters and five brothers; he was formally educated through the fourth grade; by the time he was fifteen years old, he was caring for animals and working the land; in 1953, he enlisted in the bracero program; as a bracero, he labored in the fields of Colorado, Nebraska, Texas, and Wyoming; he continued working with the program until 1958. <br/>
<br/>
Summary of Interview:  Mr. Tellez recalls his hometown, going to school, and working with his father; in 1953, he was able to get on the list of eligible workers in order to enlist in the bracero program; he went through the contracting center in Chihuahua, Chihuahua, México; from there he was transported by train to El Paso, Texas, where he was medically examined, which including being stripped and deloused; the men who fumigated the braceros, however, wore uniforms for protection as well as masks; while at the processing center in Rio Vista, he was fed eggs, beans, and tortillas; before leaving for his worksite, he was given a sack lunch consisting of two sandwiches and an orange; as a bracero, he labored in the fields of Colorado, Nebraska, Texas, and Wyoming, and he continued working with the program until 1958; he goes on to detail housing, living arrangements, amenities, provisions, daily routines, work duties, payments, remittances, and recreational activities; his longest contract was in Pecos, Texas, where he worked with roughly five or six other braceros; they made their own food, and each paid about $5.00 per week for their share of food; a few years after becoming a bracero he married a woman from his hometown; he briefly describes their relationship, including correspondence while he was in the United States, and their six children; overall, he has positive memories of the program; one of his daughters later helped him become a legal resident.<br/>
