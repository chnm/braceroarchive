        My father, Domingo Ruiz, Sr., was 12 when in 1915 he walked completely alone <br/>
        from San Luiz Potosi, Mexico, then a small village, and now the capital of a <br/>
        state in Mexico by the same name, to San Antonio, Texas. The trip was dangerous <br/>
        from many aspects: There was hardly any infrastructure to speak of at the time, <br/>
        the area was full of bandits robbing the various mines in the region, and the <br/>
        Mexican Revolution was in full force.  My father inadvertently went northeast, <br/>
        instead of due north, and had to cross first the treacherous Panuco river that <br/>
        traverses a deep tropical valley at the edge of the plateau on which the state <br/>
        sits, and then the formidable Sierra Madre. He had no money on his journey, and <br/>
        he knew no one in San Antonio when he arrived.   My father died in an automobile accident at a relatively young age, but not before leaving a family firmly established in this country. <br/>
